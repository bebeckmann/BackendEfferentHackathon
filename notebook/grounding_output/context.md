Sepsis-3  criteria  for  identifying  septic  patients  have been extensively questioned since its publication [12, 15]. A possible decrease in awareness of sepsis and a need for massive educational programs to healthcare providers are among the main concerns. In this context, how Sepsis-3 criteria would perform for sepsis detection, especially in countries with high mortality rates, still needs validation. Our study was not designed to assess Sepsis-2 or Sepsis-3 as a screening tool, since only patients admitted to ICU were included. The role of Sepsis-2 criteria for identifying septic patients is undeniable, as was the case in a recent before-after  study  conducted  in  Brazil  [28].  However, its  accuracy  to  stratify  severity  can  be  called  into  question, as demonstrated by our data. Accordingly, our study clearly shows a better discriminative performance of Sepsis-3 in predicting ICU mortality.


# Answer

It was important to test Sepsis-3 definitions outside high-income countries because these definitions had only been validated in high-income settings, and there were concerns about their performance in different contexts, particularly in countries with high mortality rates. The study aimed to assess the accuracy of Sepsis-3 criteria in stratifying mortality in a Brazilian public intensive care unit (ICU) and to investigate whether the addition of lactate values would improve this stratification [1], [2].
